=== 01 Cold open
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Aight, so check it out. May 30th, 1975. Right after midnight, in Eugene, Oregon.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] A little gold MG convertible comin' down Skyline Boulevard… crosses the center line… hits a rock wall… and flips over.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And trapped under that car? The greatest distance runner America ever had. Twenty-four years old.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] He held every American record from two thousand meters all the way to ten thousand. And just a few hours before that… he won a race.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] This the story of Steve Prefontaine. Lemme tell y'all.

=== 02 Coos Bay
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Pre was born January 25th, 1951, in Coos Bay, Oregon. Small logging town on the coast. Hard-workin' people.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] At Marshfield High, his coach was Walt McClure. And by his junior and senior years? Pre was undefeated. Cross country and track. State titles.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] His senior year he ran the mile in four oh six point nine. Oregon high school record. Then he ran the two mile in eight forty-one point five. That was reported as a national high school record.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] After high school, he stayed in Oregon. He went to the University of Oregon… to run for Bill Bowerman. The same Bill Bowerman that co-founded Nike.

=== 03 Oregon
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And at Oregon, man… Pre was on a whole 'nother level.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Seven NCAA titles. Three in cross country, four on the track. He was the first athlete ever to win four NCAA titles in the same event.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] He never lost a NCAA race longer than a mile. Not one. In four years, the only times he lost a college race in Eugene? Three times. All in the mile.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And it wasn't just that he won. It was how he won. Pre didn't sit back and kick. He went to the front and dared you to come with him. He said it himself: "No one will ever win a 5,000-meter race by runnin' an easy first two miles. Not against me."

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Hayward Field would be packed just to watch him run. That was his house.

=== 04 Munich 1972
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] 1972. He twenty-one years old, and he goin' to the Olympics in Munich. At the Trials in Eugene, he set the American record in the five thousand. Thirteen twenty-two point eight.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] September 10th, the Olympic final. The first two miles? Slow. Real slow. And that ain't Pre's race. So with a mile to go, he take the lead and he start pushin'. Hard.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] But Lasse Virén from Finland was right there. Virén go by him on the last turn. Then Mohamed Gammoudi. And then… about ten meters from the line… Ian Stewart pass him too.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Pre finish fourth. Thirteen twenty-eight point four. No medal. Point eight of a second from bronze.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] He said, "That was the most disappointed I have ever been."

=== 05 Fighting the system
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] So he come back home with one thing on his mind. Montreal. 1976.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] But see, back then, track was supposed to be "amateur." The AAU ran the sport, and if you got paid, or got endorsements, they could take your amateur status. Pre was takin' free shoes and gear from Nike. And he was loud about how unfair it all was. He went to war with the AAU.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Meanwhile? He just kept breakin' records. By 1975, he held every American record from two thousand meters to ten thousand. Seven of 'em. The five thousand? Thirteen twenty-one point nine, in Helsinki. The ten thousand? Twenty-seven forty-three point six, right there in Eugene.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And here go somethin' people don't know. Pre started goin' to the Oregon State Penitentiary to teach the inmates how to run. That prison run club he helped start? It's still goin' today.

=== 06 The last night
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] May 29th, 1975. Hayward Field. A crowd show up to watch Pre run the five thousand. And he win it. Thirteen twenty-three point eight. Frank Shorter, the Olympic marathon champ, finish second.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] After the meet, everybody go to a party at Geoff Hollister's house. Right after midnight, Pre drive Frank Shorter over to Kenny Moore's place.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Then he head home alone… down Skyline Boulevard, by Hendricks Park.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] His MG cross the center line, jump the curb, hit a rock wall, and flip. Pre was trapped underneath. They pronounced him dead right there. Twenty-four years old.

=== 07 The questions
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Now, this where it get messy.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] The police said his blood alcohol was point one six. Back then in Oregon, point one five was the legal limit.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] But his friends and his family been questionin' that for fifty years. Some of 'em believe another car ran him off the road. The police ruled that out.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Either way… the result was the same. Pre was gone. And he never got his shot at Montreal.

=== 08 Legacy
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] They held a meet in Eugene in his honor that same summer. And it's still goin' today. The Prefontaine Classic.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] The spot where he crashed is called Pre's Rock now. People still leave their race bibs and their medals there.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] They made two whole movies about him. Prefontaine in 1997, Without Limits in '98. Nike put his name on a buildin' at their headquarters.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Twenty-four years old. Every American record from two K to ten K. One Olympic final. And one question that ain't never gon' get answered.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] If Pre made it to Montreal in '76… was he winnin' gold? Lemme know in the comments.
