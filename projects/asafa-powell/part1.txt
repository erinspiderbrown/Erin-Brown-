Asafa Powell ran under ten seconds in the hundred ninety-seven times. That's more than anybody in history. He broke the world record. Then he tied it. Then he tied it again. Then he broke it again. And he never won an individual Olympic or world title. Not one. So how does the fastest man on the planet keep losing the only races that matter? Let me tell you the story.

Asafa grew up in Jamaica. He was the youngest of six boys, and both of his parents were ministers. In 2001 he starts training with coach Stephen Francis at MVP, and he starts getting fast. Like, scary fast.

But life hits him before the track does. In 2002, his older brother Michael is shot and killed in a taxi in New York. About a year later, another brother, Vaughn, dies of a heart attack. Two brothers, gone, right as his career is starting.

And then his first World Championships. Paris, 2003. He doesn't even make it to the final. He false starts in the quarterfinal, and he's disqualified. Just like that.

2004. The Athens Olympics. Asafa comes in as one of the fastest men in the world. He makes the final, and he finishes fifth. Nine point nine four. No medal.

Now watch what happens when there's no pressure. June 2005, he's back in Athens, at a regular meet. Nine point seven seven. New world record. Asafa Powell is the fastest man alive.

Then a groin injury takes him out of the 2005 World Championships. He doesn't even get to run.

2006, there's no Olympics and no World Championships. So what does he do? He ties his own world record in Gateshead. Nine point seven seven. Then he ties it again in Zurich. Nine point seven seven. He wins the Commonwealth Games. Everybody is saying the same thing: next year is his year.

2007, World Championships in Osaka. This is it. And Tyson Gay runs him down. Tyson wins. Asafa's own cousin, Derrick Atkins, running for the Bahamas, takes silver. Asafa gets bronze. And afterwards he says something you never hear from a world record holder. He said, "When Tyson came on and gave me a little bit of pressure, I just panicked." And Michael Johnson, one of the greatest sprinters ever, said you could see it on his face. He said you could see him thinking, "I'm losing it, I'm losing it," and he just gave up.

Now get this. Two weeks later, at a meet in Rieti, Italy, he runs nine point seven four. New world record. Again. The fastest time ever, two weeks after he couldn't win the race that counted.
