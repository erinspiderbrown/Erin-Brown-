2008. Beijing. This is supposed to be the one. That year, Asafa becomes the first man in history to run under ten seconds fifteen times in a single season. Fifteen. Nobody had ever done that. But that May, a tall kid named Usain Bolt takes his world record. And in the Olympic final, Bolt runs nine point six nine and changes the sport forever. Asafa? Fifth. Again. Nine point nine five. He said it himself: "I've got no idea why I'm always winning on the circuit, but then finishing fifth at the Olympics." And about two weeks later? He runs nine point seven two in Lausanne. The fastest he would ever run. After the Olympics. Again.

He did win a relay gold in Beijing. Hold on to that one, because it's coming back.

2009, World Championships in Berlin. Bolt runs nine point five eight. Asafa runs nine point eight four. Bronze. Again. A few days later he anchors Jamaica's four by one hundred to gold. On a relay, with his team around him, he could get it done. Alone, in a final, it never came.

2011, Daegu. His groin goes again. He pulls out before the hundred even starts.

2012, the London Olympics. He makes the final. In the race, his groin gives out. He pulls up and jogs across the line in eleven point nine nine. Last place, in his third Olympic final.

2013. It gets worse. He tests positive for a banned stimulant called oxilofrine. He says it came from a supplement, that he never knowingly took anything. He gets an eighteen-month ban. The Court of Arbitration for Sport cuts it down to six months, but the damage to his name is already done.

And remember that Beijing relay gold? In 2017, a retest catches his teammate Nesta Carter. The whole team gets disqualified. Asafa has to give the medal back, nine years later.

But here's the thing about Asafa Powell. He never stopped showing up. At thirty-two, he makes another World Championship final, in 2015. And in Rio, 2016, at thirty-three, he leads off Jamaica's four by one hundred in the Olympic final. Jamaica wins. Finally, an Olympic gold medal that nobody can take from him.

He retired in 2022, at forty. Ninety-seven sub-ten races, the most of all time. Four world records, counting the ties. Of every man who has ever run nine point seven two or faster, he's the only one who never won an individual Olympic or world title in the hundred. The only one.

So was it nerves? Injuries? Or was he just born in the wrong era, right next to the greatest sprinter who ever lived? Because when the lights were off, Asafa Powell was the fastest man in the world. When the lights came on, he couldn't find that same race.
